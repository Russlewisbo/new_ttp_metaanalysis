## 12. Review question

In adults with bloodstream infection (bacteremia or fungemia), is a shorter blood culture time-to-positivity (TTP), compared to a longer TTP, associated with higher all-cause mortality and a higher risk of persistent bacteremia?

---

## 13. Searches

Electronic database searches will be run in: PubMed (MEDLINE), Embase (Elsevier embase.com), Cochrane CENTRAL (Wiley Cochrane Library), Web of Science Core Collection (Clarivate), CINAHL Complete (EBSCOhost), and Scopus (Elsevier).

The search strategy combines two concept blocks: (1) bloodstream infection / bacteremia / fungemia / blood culture / sepsis terms (MeSH/Emtree and free-text with truncation for British and American spellings) AND (2) time-to-positivity exposure terms (free-text only, as no controlled vocabulary term exists for this concept), including "time to positivity", "time to blood culture positivity", "time to detection", "growth detection time", "differential time to positivity", "DTP", "DTTP", and "TTP" tethered to a relevant context term to exclude irrelevant homonyms (thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, triphosphate compounds). No outcome block is applied, in order to maximize sensitivity for prognostic-factor studies whose outcomes may be reported as secondary findings and may not be reliably MeSH-indexed.

Searches will be limited to human studies. No language restriction will be applied at the search level; language at the eligibility stage will permit English, Italian, Spanish, French, German, and Portuguese full texts (translation capability available within the review team). No date restriction will be applied at the lower bound; the upper bound will be the date the search is executed (to be re-run within 6 months of manuscript submission).

Supplementary searches will include: ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP, medRxiv, bioRxiv, forward citation tracking in Web of Science and Scopus from two seed publications (Hsieh 2022, BMC Infect Dis; the Jan 2026 IJMPR meta-analysis), reference-list hand-searches of all included studies and recent narrative reviews, and direct correspondence with first or senior authors of key studies regarding unpublished or in-press work.

Full search strings for each database, with line-numbered concept blocks, are appended to this protocol and will be reported in the supplementary materials per PRISMA-S.

## 15. Condition or domain being studied

Bloodstream infection (bacteremia and fungemia) in adult patients, with focus on the prognostic value of blood culture time-to-positivity for adverse clinical outcomes.

---

## 16. Participants / population

**Inclusion:** Adult patients (≥18 years, or studies where ≥80% of the sample is ≥18 years) with culture-confirmed bloodstream infection of bacterial or fungal origin, regardless of clinical setting (community-onset, healthcare-associated, hospital-acquired, ICU, ward, or emergency department). Studies enrolling patients with monomicrobial or polymicrobial bacteremia/fungemia are eligible.

**Exclusion:** Pediatric-only populations (>20% of sample <18 years); studies restricted to contaminant or commensal-only bloodstream cultures; studies of mycobacterial bloodstream infection only (different growth kinetics); studies of viral bloodstream infection; studies in which TTP is studied only as a diagnostic marker (e.g., catheter-related bloodstream infection diagnosis using paired peripheral and catheter blood cultures with differential TTP) without a clinical outcome assessment.

---

## 17. Intervention(s), exposure(s)

The exposure is blood culture time-to-positivity (TTP), defined as the interval from initiation of blood culture incubation to detection of microbial growth by the automated continuous-monitoring blood culture system. Studies must report TTP either as a continuous variable (mean, median, or regression coefficient per unit time) or dichotomized at a study-defined threshold (cutpoint), or in ≥3 ordinal categories. Both BD BACTEC and bioMérieux BacT/ALERT systems are eligible.

Studies reporting time-to-positivity of urine, sputum, wound, or other non-blood specimens are not eligible.

---

## 18. Comparator(s) / control

For dichotomized analyses: longer TTP (above the study-defined cutpoint). For continuous analyses: the unit-increase comparator (typically per-hour increase in TTP).

When a study reports multiple cutpoints, the cutpoint specified by the authors as primary will be used; if no primary cutpoint is specified, the cutpoint closest to the median TTP of the study cohort will be used, with the alternatives examined in sensitivity analysis.

---

## 19. Types of study to be included

Prospective cohort studies, retrospective cohort studies, nested case-control studies, and case-control studies with a clearly defined source population. Cross-sectional studies are eligible only if TTP is measured before outcome ascertainment.

Excluded: case reports, case series with fewer than 20 patients, narrative reviews, conference abstracts without full data (full conference proceedings papers are eligible), editorials, letters without primary data, simulation studies, in vitro studies, studies in non-human subjects.

---

## 20. Context

Any geographical setting and any healthcare setting (community, hospital ward, intensive care unit, emergency department, long-term care). All study designs from any country are eligible if they meet the population and exposure criteria.

---

## 21. Main outcome(s)

**Primary outcome 1:** All-cause mortality, assessed at the longest follow-up reported by each study within 90 days of index blood culture. Where multiple time-points are reported, 30-day mortality will be preferred; in-hospital mortality will be used when no 30-day estimate is available.

**Primary outcome 2:** Persistent bacteremia, defined as positive follow-up blood cultures at ≥48 hours after initiation of effective antimicrobial therapy. Where studies use different operational definitions (e.g., persistent ≥3 days, persistent ≥7 days, microbiological clearance failure), these will be harmonized to the "persistent bacteremia" outcome class and the definition heterogeneity examined in sensitivity analysis.

Effect measures will be expressed as odds ratios with 95% credible intervals (Bayesian primary) and 95% confidence intervals (frequentist sensitivity), with shorter TTP as the index exposure. Hazard ratios and risk ratios reported in primary studies will be retained on their native scale where possible and harmonized to log-odds-ratios for pooling, with HR-to-OR conversion flagged and examined in sensitivity analysis. Prediction intervals will be reported alongside credible intervals.

---

## 22. Additional outcome(s)

- Recurrent bacteremia (re-positive blood culture after documented clearance and within 90 days of index episode)
- Sepsis-related severity outcomes (need for vasopressors, mechanical ventilation, ICU admission) — reported descriptively
- Length of hospital stay (descriptive only)
- Endocarditis as a complication (descriptive only)

These additional outcomes will be reported if data are sufficient (≥4 studies per outcome) and will not be pooled if data quality issues prevent reliable synthesis.

---

## 23. Data extraction (selection and coding)

Titles and abstracts will be screened independently by two reviewers using Covidence. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or, if needed, by a third reviewer (RE Lewis as senior arbitrator). Full texts of potentially eligible studies will be retrieved and screened independently by the same two reviewers. Inter-rater agreement at the full-text stage will be reported as Cohen's kappa.

Data extraction will be performed using a piloted relational database (Excel workbook with linked tables) containing the following sheets: `tbl_study` (study metadata), `tbl_population` (population characteristics), `tbl_ttp` (TTP measurement details), `tbl_outcomes` (outcome data including 2×2 tables and reported effect estimates), and a Codebook. Two reviewers will extract data independently for at least 20% of included studies, with discrepancies adjudicated by discussion; full dual extraction will be performed if disagreement on the pilot sample exceeds 10% of fields.

Extracted variables will include: study identifiers, design, country, enrollment period, single- vs multi-center status, sample size, age (mean/median and dispersion), sex distribution, pathogen class and species, infection source, illness severity scores (APACHE II, SOFA, Pitt, Charlson), proportion receiving appropriate empiric antimicrobial therapy, source-control status, blood culture system, TTP reporting format (continuous, dichotomized, categorical), TTP summary statistics, dichotomization cutpoint and its basis (ROC-derived, clinical convention, prior literature, median split), 2×2 tables linking TTP category to outcome, reported adjusted and unadjusted effect estimates with confidence intervals, and the list of adjustment variables.

Authors of primary studies will be contacted by email for missing data items considered critical to the primary analyses (2×2 cell counts, cutpoint definitions, denominators); up to two reminders will be sent at 2-week intervals.

---

## 24. Risk of bias (quality) assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed using the Quality In Prognosis Studies (QUIPS) tool (Hayden et al. 2013), which is the recommended instrument for prognostic factor reviews and addresses six domains: (1) study participation, (2) study attrition, (3) prognostic factor measurement, (4) outcome measurement, (5) study confounding, and (6) statistical analysis and reporting.

Each domain will be rated as low, moderate, or high risk by two reviewers independently. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or third-reviewer adjudication. Domain ratings will be summarized into an overall risk-of-bias judgement per study using the GRADE prognostic factor guidance (Huguet et al. 2013). Inter-rater agreement on the overall judgement will be reported.

The certainty of evidence for each primary outcome will be assessed using the GRADE framework adapted for prognostic factor reviews (high, moderate, low, very low), considering risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias.

---

## 25. Strategy for data synthesis

**Primary analysis.** Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis will be performed in R using the `brms` package with the cmdstanr backend (Stan). The model specification is:

```
yi | se(sei) ~ 1 + (1 | study_id)
```

with priors: intercept (pooled log-odds-ratio) ~ Normal(0, 1) — 95% prior mass on OR 0.14 to 7.4; between-study standard deviation τ ~ Half-Cauchy(0, 0.5). Models will be run with 4 chains, 4000 iterations, 1000 warmup, adapt_delta = 0.95. Convergence will be assessed via R-hat (<1.01), bulk and tail ESS (>400), and divergent transition count.

Effect sizes will be computed as follows: from 2×2 tables, using `metafor::escalc()` with the standard log-odds-ratio formula; from reported adjusted or unadjusted OR/HR/RR with confidence intervals, via back-calculation `yi = log(estimate)`, `sei = (log(ci_upper) − log(ci_lower)) / 3.92`. Adjusted estimates will be preferred when both adjusted and unadjusted are reported. Continuous-TTP studies (effect per hour) will be analysed in a separate model and not combined with dichotomized studies in the primary pooling; secondary dose-response modeling will be explored.

**Frequentist comparison.** Random-effects meta-analysis using REML (restricted maximum likelihood) in `metafor::rma()` will be run as a sensitivity check, with results reported alongside the Bayesian primary.

**Heterogeneity.** Tau-squared, I-squared, and 95% prediction intervals will be reported. Heterogeneity will be considered substantial if I-squared > 50% or τ > 0.30 on the log-OR scale.

**Publication bias.** Funnel plot inspection, Egger's regression test, trim-and-fill (Duval & Tweedie), frequentist step-function selection models (`metafor::selmodel()`), and a fully Bayesian selection model implemented in Stan with both uniform and skeptical priors on the publication-selection parameter δ will be applied. Where these methods diverge, all results will be reported transparently.

**Prior sensitivity.** The primary analysis will be re-run under alternative priors: Intercept ~ Normal(0, 0.5) (skeptical), Intercept ~ Normal(0, 2) (broad), τ ~ Half-Normal(0, 0.5) (tighter), τ ~ Half-Cauchy(0, 1) (broader). Prior sensitivity will be quantified using power-scaling diagnostics via the `priorsense` package.

**Influence diagnostics.** Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses will be reported for the primary outcome, with attention to studies contributing disproportionate influence on the pooled estimate or the heterogeneity variance.

## 26. Analysis of subgroups or subsets

The following pre-specified subgroup and meta-regression analyses will be performed for the primary outcome (mortality), conditional on ≥3 studies per subgroup category:

- Pathogen class (gram-positive vs gram-negative)
- *Staphylococcus aureus* vs all other pathogens
- TTP cutpoint (continuous moderator, hours) — hypothesised to be the dominant source of heterogeneity based on preliminary work showing approximately −0.03 log-OR per hour increase in cutpoint
- Blood culture detection system (BacT/ALERT vs BACTEC)
- Risk of bias rating (low vs moderate vs high)
- Adjusted vs unadjusted effect estimates
- Publication year (continuous, to detect temporal trends)
- Geographic region (Europe, North America, Asia, other)

Subgroup analyses for persistent bacteremia will be limited by the smaller number of studies and reported descriptively where pooling is not feasible.

## 34. Details of any existing review of the same topic by the same authors

The present review is a methodologically strengthened re-execution of a 2025 University of Padova School of Medicine dissertation by F. Pangallo, supervised by RE Lewis. The dissertation included a preliminary literature search (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, CINAHL, Web of Science), screening and full-text review by a single reviewer, descriptive data extraction, and exploratory Bayesian and frequentist meta-analyses. The dissertation was not published or formally registered. The present review supersedes the dissertation and addresses methodological limitations including single-reviewer screening, a non-validated risk-of-bias rubric, and an over-restrictive search strategy.

Two previously published systematic reviews on related questions are noted: Hsieh et al. 2022 (BMC Infect Dis, 24 studies, S. aureus bacteremia focus) and a January 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis published in the International Journal of Medical and Pharmaceutical Research. The present review differs from these by (1) using a Bayesian analytical framework with full posterior inference, (2) examining TTP cutpoint as a quantitative moderator of effect size, (3) including a larger study corpus, and (4) applying multiple complementary publication-bias adjustment methods including a fully Bayesian selection model.
