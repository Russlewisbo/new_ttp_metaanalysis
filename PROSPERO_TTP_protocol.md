## 15. Review question(s)

**Primary:** In patients with microbiologically confirmed bloodstream infection (BSI), is a short blood culture time to positivity (TTP) associated with increased all-cause mortality (in-hospital or 30-day)?

**Secondary:**
1. Is short TTP associated with persistent bacteremia (failure of microbiological clearance despite appropriate therapy)?
2. Is the prognostic value of TTP modified by pathogen class (Gram-positive vs. Gram-negative), the TTP cutpoint used, study risk of bias, or year of publication?

## 18. Condition or domain being studied

Bloodstream infections (bacteremia, fungemia, candidemia) and sepsis with positive blood culture, in any patient population, any geographic setting, any pathogen.

## 19. Participants/population

**Inclusion:** Adult or pediatric patients with microbiologically confirmed bloodstream infection in whom TTP was measured using an automated continuous-monitoring blood culture system (e.g., BACTEC FX, BacT/ALERT VIRTUO, VersaTREK).

**Exclusion:** Studies that did not use automated continuous-monitoring systems for TTP measurement; studies in which TTP referred exclusively to non-blood culture specimens; populations consisting exclusively of mycobacteremia.

## 20. Intervention(s), exposure(s)

Short blood culture time to positivity. TTP is defined as the elapsed time from the start of incubation in an automated blood culture system to the system's detection of microbial growth. Studies will be included regardless of whether TTP is analyzed as a continuous variable or dichotomized at a study-defined cutpoint.

## 21. Comparator(s)/control

Long TTP, defined either as TTP above the study-specific cutpoint where dichotomized, or as the comparison of longer versus shorter values where TTP is analyzed continuously.

## 22. Types of study to be included

**Included:** Prospective cohort studies; retrospective cohort studies; case-control and nested case-control studies; case-cohort studies; cross-sectional studies with prognostic analysis; randomized controlled trials reporting TTP and clinical outcomes.

**Excluded:** Case reports and case series with fewer than 10 patients; editorials, commentaries, and narrative reviews; conference abstracts without sufficient quantitative data; existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses (used only as sources for citation tracking and reference checking); studies that report TTP only as a continuous variable without sufficient data to derive an effect estimate; studies with insufficient outcome data to calculate or extract an OR, HR, or RR with confidence interval, or to construct a 2×2 contingency table.

## 23. Context

Hospitalized patients in any setting (ICU, general ward, hematology/oncology unit, transplant unit, emergency department, outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy programs). No geographic, healthcare-system, or income-level restrictions.

## 24. Main outcome(s)

All-cause mortality, defined as in-hospital mortality or 30-day mortality, whichever was reported by the primary study. Where both were available, in-hospital mortality will be preferred for primary analysis and 30-day mortality used in sensitivity analysis.

## 25. Measures of effect

Odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval. Where studies report hazard ratios (HR) or risk ratios (RR) with 95% CI, these will be extracted directly and treated as approximating the OR for low-event-rate outcomes, with sensitivity analyses to assess the impact of mixing effect measures. Where only 2×2 contingency data are available, ORs will be calculated using the Mantel-Haenszel method with 0.5 continuity correction for zero cells (escalc() in the metafor R package). All effect estimates will be converted to log-OR with associated standard errors for pooling. Adjusted estimates will be preferred over unadjusted when both are reported. Studies will be summarized in the standard meta-analytic format: pooled OR (95% credible interval for Bayesian models / 95% confidence interval for frequentist models), τ², I², and prediction interval.

## 26. Additional outcome(s)

Persistent bacteremia, defined as failure of microbiological clearance with blood cultures persistently positive despite appropriate antimicrobial therapy (typically >48–72 hours, per study-specific definition).

## 27. Measures of effect (additional outcomes)

Same as for the primary outcome.

## 28. Data extraction (selection and coding)

Title and abstract screening, and full-text review, performed independently by two reviewers using Covidence systematic review software (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia). Disagreements resolved by discussion, with third-reviewer adjudication where consensus cannot be reached. Inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ) will be calculated and reported for both screening stages.

Data extraction is performed into a structured relational database with four linked tables joined by a unique study identifier:
- **Study metadata:** first author, publication year, country, study design, single- vs. multi-center, risk of bias rating.
- **Population:** sample size, age category, pathogen class (Gram-positive, Gram-negative, mixed, fungal), specific pathogen(s), source of infection, severity-of-illness indicators where reported.
- **TTP measurement:** blood culture system used, cutpoint definition and value (in hours), TTP reporting format (continuous, dichotomous, categorical).
- **Outcomes:** outcome definition (mortality timepoint; persistent bacteremia definition), 2×2 contingency data, reported effect estimates (OR/HR/RR) with 95% CI, source (adjusted or unadjusted), covariates adjusted for.

Where data are missing or ambiguous, corresponding authors will be contacted by email. The extraction database and screening decisions will be deposited on the Open Science Framework at the time of publication.

## 29. Risk of bias (quality) assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed using the **QUIPS (Quality In Prognosis Studies)** tool (Hayden et al., Ann Intern Med 2013), which was developed specifically for prognostic factor studies and is recommended by the Cochrane Prognosis Methods Group. QUIPS evaluates six domains: (1) study participation, (2) study attrition, (3) prognostic factor measurement, (4) outcome measurement, (5) study confounding, and (6) statistical analysis and reporting. Each domain is rated as low, moderate, or high risk of bias, with an overall study rating derived per QUIPS guidance. Two reviewers will independently assess each study; disagreements will be resolved by consensus or third-reviewer adjudication.

*Note for protocol disclosure:* the master's thesis on which this review is based used an adapted Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. The shift to QUIPS reflects current methodological consensus for prognostic factor reviews and will be re-applied to the full study set.

The certainty of evidence will be assessed using the GRADE framework for prognostic factor studies (Huguet et al., Syst Rev 2013).

## 30. Strategy for data synthesis

**Primary analysis — Bayesian hierarchical random-effects meta-analysis.** Implemented in R (version 4.5.1 or later) using the brms package (Bürkner 2017) with the Stan/CmdStan backend. Model specification:

```
log(OR_i) | SE(SE_i) ~ 1 + (1 | study_id)
```

where log(OR_i) is the observed log-odds ratio for study i, SE_i is the associated standard error, and (1 | study_id) is a random intercept capturing between-study heterogeneity.

**Prior specification (weakly informative):**
- Overall effect μ on the log-OR scale: Normal(0, 1). Places 95% of prior mass on OR between 0.14 and 7.4.
- Between-study standard deviation τ: Half-Cauchy(0, 0.5), consistent with Gelman (2006) for variance components in hierarchical models.

**MCMC sampling:** Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, 4 parallel chains, 4000 iterations per chain (1000 warm-up), target acceptance rate 0.95. Convergence assessed by R-hat (< 1.01 required for all parameters), bulk and tail effective sample size (> 1000 required), and visual inspection of trace plots. Divergent transitions reported.

**Posterior summaries:** Pooled OR with 95% credible interval, posterior probability of harmful effect (P(OR > 1)), between-study τ with 95% CrI, and 95% prediction interval.

**Frequentist validation analysis.** Random-effects models using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation will be fit using the metafor package as a validation cross-check. Between-study heterogeneity quantified by τ² and I².

**Publication bias.** Assessed using multiple complementary methods:
- Visual inspection of funnel plots
- Egger's regression test for funnel asymmetry
- Duval and Tweedie trim-and-fill method
- Frequentist step-function selection models (PET-PEESE and three-parameter selection models)
- Bayesian step-function selection models implemented in Stan, with two prior specifications for the selection parameter δ: non-informative Uniform(0, 1) and skeptical Beta(2, 5) corresponding to a prior mean of moderate selection bias

## 31. Analysis of subgroups or subsets

Pre-specified Bayesian univariable meta-regressions will examine the following moderators of the primary mortality effect:
- Pathogen class (Gram-positive vs. Gram-negative)
- TTP cutpoint (continuous, in hours)
- Risk of bias rating (low vs. moderate/high)
- Year of publication (continuous)

Moderator coefficients will be modeled with Normal(0, 0.5) priors providing moderate regularization. R² explained by each moderator will be reported.

**Stratified subgroup analyses** by pathogen class (Gram-positive, Gram-negative) will pool effect sizes within each stratum and compare 95% credible intervals for the difference in pooled log-OR.

**Sensitivity analyses:**
- Leave-one-out diagnostic
- Prior sensitivity: comparison of primary priors with (a) wide Normal(0, 2.5), (b) skeptical Normal(0, 0.5), and (c) half-Normal alternative for τ
- Restriction to studies at low risk of bias
- Restriction by source of effect estimate (adjusted-only vs. unadjusted-only)
- Cumulative meta-analysis by publication year
- Exclusion of statistical outliers identified by influence diagnostics
